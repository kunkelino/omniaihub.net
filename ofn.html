If you're on the street right now, here are a few tips that can help.

  • Go to your local library. Libraries are more than just books. They're a place to stay cool in the summer, warm in the winter, charge your phone, use free Wi-Fi, search for jobs, and find local resources.
  • Laundry Love — find local laundromats that will let you do laundry for free or low cost.
  • Massachusetts School of Barbering — low-cost haircuts provided by barber students.
  • Reach out to churches. Even if they can't provide direct help, many can point you toward shelters, food pantries, clothing closets, transportation assistance, and other local programs.
  • Call 311. If you don't have an ID, ask what resources are available to help you obtain one.
  • Keep your important documents safe. If possible, store your ID, Social Security card, birth certificate, and other important papers in a waterproof bag or folder.
  • Don't be afraid to ask for help. There are people and organizations that genuinely want to help you get back on your feet.
  • Talk to your caseworker or social worker. Ask for a referral to Salvation Army and Goodwill for clothing.
  • Lost your ID? Talk to your case manager or social worker, or call 311 for assistance getting a new ID, Social Security card, or birth certificate.
